Revenue screenshots travel fast because they compress a story into one seductive image.

The problem is that the image usually hides the details that matter most.

## 1. Ask what the metric actually is

Is the screenshot showing revenue, profit, cash collected, annualized run rate, or a dashboard projection?

Those numbers are not interchangeable.

## 2. Ask what the time window hides

A business with a strong month can still have weak retention, high refund rates, or a traffic spike that does not repeat.

Without the time window, the screenshot can make a fragile business look durable.

## 3. Ask what costs do not appear

A top-line number says nothing about ad spend, contractors, software costs, chargebacks, or the founder's time.

A useful revenue teardown should move from “this screenshot exists” to “this business model makes sense.”

## 4. Ask whether the growth source is durable

Did the sales come from a launch, one influencer mention, a community audience, or a real compounding acquisition channel?

That answer changes whether the business is interesting for imitation.

The goal is not to be cynical. The goal is to read money claims with enough structure that the archive becomes genuinely useful.
