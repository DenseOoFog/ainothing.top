# AInothing.top writing templates

This folder-level guide keeps the blog consistent as the archive grows.

## Positioning in one sentence
AInothing.top uses clickable money headlines to pull readers into grounded teardowns of AI and internet businesses, with a strong bias toward repeatability, operating mechanics, and skepticism.

## Editorial rule of thumb
Every post should answer at least one of these questions clearly:
- What was sold?
- Where did demand come from?
- How did conversion happen?
- Which part is durable, fragile, or founder-specific?

## Title formulas by category

### Revenue Breakdowns
Use these when the article leads with a number or business outcome.
- Inside a $X/month [business model] with [specific constraint or edge]
- Breaking down a $X/month [site/product/business] without romanticizing [channel]
- What a $X/month [operator/business] really depends on
- How to read a $X/month [business model] without confusing [vanity metric] for a business
- Before calling this $X/month [business] passive income, look at [constraint]

### AI Signals
Use these when the post explains a market shift.
- The AI shift that changes how [creator/operators/founders] can make money
- Why [new capability or launch] matters more for distribution than the headline suggests
- The quiet AI trend that could create the next wave of [business type]
- What changed in AI this week that actually matters for builders

### AI Tools
Use these when the post judges whether a tool creates real leverage.
- Is [tool name] actually useful for [job] or just good marketing?
- What [tool name] is good at, bad at, and not worth using for
- How to evaluate [tool category] without getting distracted by demos
- The real workflow advantage inside [tool/product claim]

### Growth Playbooks
Use these when the article focuses on traffic, conversion, or audience systems.
- The growth loop behind [business type] is simpler than it looks
- How [site/newsletter/product] compounds when [system] is done right
- What actually moves growth for a small [business type]
- The distribution playbook behind [result], minus the fantasy

### Solo Ops
Use these when the post focuses on a one-person or tiny-team constraint.
- What breaks first in a one-person [business type]
- The solo-operator version of [business model] only works if [constraint]
- How one person can run [business model] without becoming the bottleneck
- The hidden labor behind a “simple” one-person business

## Flagship revenue breakdown template
Use this for the weekly anchor piece.

### Article structure
1. Hook
   - Open with the number and one sentence defining what the number really is.
   - Example: MRR, annualized revenue run rate, services billings, sponsorship pipeline, or gross marketplace GMV.
2. What was sold
   - Product type
   - Price point
   - Buyer type
   - Monetization layers
3. Where demand came from
   - Search, social, community, outbound, referrals, partnerships, or audience carryover
4. How conversion happened
   - Landing page, sales motion, demo, pricing structure, bundles, email flow, or trust assets
5. What the business quality looks like
   - Margin, retention, concentration risk, founder dependency, complexity, delivery load
6. Can a normal operator repeat it?
   - Name the real bottleneck directly
7. Final verdict
   - Durable, fragile, or conditionally repeatable

## Supporting article templates

### AI Signals post
1. What changed?
2. Why does it matter for builders or publishers?
3. Which business models benefit first?
4. What should readers watch over the next 30 to 90 days?

### AI Tools post
1. What does the tool promise?
2. Which workflow does it genuinely improve?
3. Where does it break down in practice?
4. Who should ignore it?
5. Final recommendation

### Growth Playbook post
1. Define the growth problem
2. Show the core loop
3. Explain the leverage point
4. Call out the likely failure mode
5. Give a practical “do this next” checklist

### Solo Ops post
1. Define the business model
2. Show where time goes
3. Explain what can be systemized
4. Explain what still depends on the founder
5. Give an honest sustainability verdict

## Sourcing checklist for revenue posts
- Can the revenue claim be linked to a credible source?
- Is the number revenue, profit, GMV, billings, or ARR?
- Is the timeframe monthly, annual, launch-based, or cumulative?
- What costs or delivery burdens are hidden?
- What risk is missing from the screenshot?

## End every strong post with one of these verdicts
- Repeatable if the reader can build the same distribution asset.
- Interesting, but mostly founder-specific.
- Good business, weak screenshot bait.
- Strong headline, fragile economics.
- Worth studying for the funnel, not for the exact number.
